The 49 hidden monitor identities are not counted as 49 launch products. They are implementation/use-case backlog beneath the one Clarity monitoring service.

## Readiness
The 15 public watches have public routes and tested monitoring engines, but the repository still describes the environment as pre-launch and Free-only. Distribution therefore classifies them as **Working V1 / QA Required**, not externally launched.

Learn and Free Local Tools are public content/resources. Desktop remains Hold/Candidate.

## Shared distribution engines
1. Search/Learn content
2. Website/domain technical communities
3. Developer/DevOps community participation
4. Product discovery directories
5. Show HN / maker launch
6. Cross-promotion from related watch results and Learn pages

## Biggest opportunities
- Lead with the service promise, not a wall of monitor names.
- Use the website/domain cohort first because one target naturally demonstrates uptime, change, TLS, domain, and DNS history.
- Use Learn as the durable intent-capture engine.
- Use technical watches to obtain concrete developer/operations feedback.

## Biggest weaknesses
- Production stranger-flow proof is still required before promotion.
- First-party attribution is implemented but still needs deployed verification with a tagged test campaign.
- Shareable artifacts need a privacy-safe design before they are treated as a growth loop.
- The desktop roadmap is documentation, not shipped software.

## What mapping taught us
Clarity is much narrower and clearer than the early "know/decision" umbrella suggests. The real implemented center is durable public-source monitoring with evidence and history. Learn is an acquisition layer; monitor identities are implementation capabilities; local scripts are a separate utility track; desktop is a separate future direction.

No external promotion has been executed.
