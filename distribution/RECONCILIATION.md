# Portfolio reconciliation

## Canonical accounting
- Public monitoring service: **1 suite**
- Public watch capabilities: **15**
- Learn content entries: **22**
- Free Local Tools: **10 released downloadable scripts**
- Desktop portfolio: **10 candidate/roadmap products**
- Broader internal monitor catalog: **capability/backlog layer, consolidated under the service rather than counted as separate launch products**

## Readiness
The 15 public watches have public routes and tested monitoring engines, but the repository still describes the environment as pre-launch and Free-only. Distribution therefore classifies them as **Working V1 / QA Required**, not externally launched.

Learn and Free Local Tools are public content/resources. Desktop remains Hold/Candidate.

## Shared distribution engines
1. Search/Learn content
2. Website/domain technical communities
3. Developer/DevOps community participation
4. Product discovery directories
5. Show HN / maker launch
6. Cross-promotion from related watch results and Learn pages

## Biggest opportunity
The strongest simple story is not "15 monitors." It is: **watch one website/domain and preserve the evidence you would otherwise keep checking manually.**

## Biggest weakness
A new visitor can understand individual monitor pages, but external distribution will fail if the end-to-end account/follow/observation loop or attribution cannot be measured reliably. Production proof is Priority 0.

## What mapping taught us
Clarity is much narrower and clearer than the early "know/decision" umbrella suggests. The real implemented center is durable public-source monitoring with evidence and history. Learn is an acquisition layer; monitor identities are implementation capabilities; desktop is a separate future direction.

No external promotion has been executed.
